Part 1: Constitutional & Early Statutory Foundations

1. Magna Carta (1215) — The Rule of Law
Fact Verified: Establishes the constitutional baseline that justice cannot depend entirely on the personal "will" of whoever holds administrative power. Power must be balanced by accountability.
View Core Resource: British Library Magna Carta Collection →
2. Public Health Act 1875 & Model Bye-Laws (1877)
Fact Verified: The foundational legislation that unified fragmented local powers, creating the first strict national templates for structural wall thickness, drainage compliance, and minimum site sanitation margins.
View Primary Statute: UK National Archives (Legislation.gov.uk) →
3. Public Health Act 1936 (Section 64)
Fact Verified: Dictated an objective blueprint inspection mechanism that was entirely blind to the identity or status of the applicant. It operated purely for public safety and provided no financial guarantee of value to the project owner.
View Historical Statute: UK Legislation Archives →

Part 2: The Construction Trap Infrastructure

4. Defective Premises Act 1972 (Section 1)
Fact Verified: Locks strict civil liability directly onto the builder to ensure a home is fit for human habitation. The fact that an inspector signs off on the site does not absolve or diminish this personal, non-transferable legal duty.
"A person taking on work for or in connection with the provision of a dwelling... owes a duty to see that the work which he takes on is done in a workmanlike or, as the case may be, professional manner, with proper materials and so that as regards that work the dwelling will be fit for human habitation when completed."
View Active Statute: Defective Premises Act 1972 →
5. The December 1979 Policy Shift (Michael Heseltine Address to the NHBC)
Fact Verified: The formal launch of the deregulatory initiative designed to strip public expenditure, break the state inspectorate monopoly, slash the prescriptive building manual, and transition risk completely to the private market.
View Historical Analysis: University of London Press Record →
6. Building Act 1984 & Building Regulations 1985
Fact Verified: The statutory package resulting from the 1979 initiative. Introduced commercial "Approved Inspectors" (like the NHBC) and condensed prescriptive structural checks from a 306-page manual down to a 24-page framework of open "functional requirements".

Part 3: Total Immunity Case Law (The Trap Clamps Shut)

7. Murphy v Brentwood District Council [1991] 1 AC 398
Fact Verified: The landmark House of Lords precedent that completely immunized state building control. Ruled that a local authority cannot be sued in negligence for the economic cost of repairing defects before an accident occurs, completely overturning Anns (1978).
"The injury sustained by the owner is purely economic... I have reached the conclusion that Anns was wrongly decided as regards the scope of any duty of care at common law owed by local authorities... It would be a very far-reaching step to hold that a statutory regulatory body owed a common law duty of care to avoid causing purely economic loss to individuals."
— Lord Keith
View Judicial Reference Context: UK Supreme Court Records Archive →
8. Lessees of Herons Court v Heronslea Ltd [2019] EWCA Civ 1423
Fact Verified: The final common law confirmation that mirrors the council's immunity in the private sector. The Court of Appeal ruled that private Approved Inspectors (specifically including the NHBC) carry zero liability under the Defective Premises Act 1972, leaving builders entirely isolated.
"The statutory function of an Approved Inspector is to ensure compliance with the Building Regulations. Their function is regulatory; it is not to construct or design dwellings... The responsibility for ensuring the building is fit for habitation remains with those who actually do the work."
— Lord Justice Hamblen
